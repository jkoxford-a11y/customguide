A note on sourcing, since this matters

From practice pieces are language that's actually been used and tested in a real course. Suggestion pieces are ones built for this toolkit from general prompt-engineering practice — reasonable starting points, not field-tested here yet. Treating prompt fragments as recombinable blocks is a common framing in current prompt-engineering writing, but composing them doesn't guarantee predictable behavior the way physical bricks do — the same model can respond differently to the same instructions on different runs. Test whatever you assemble against a real student question before trusting it with a class.

🗂️ Your Faculty Workspace Bot

This is a different bot from the one above. The tutor bot is for students. This one is for you — course prep, drafting assessments, rewriting a syllabus section, thinking through a unit. It isn't shared with anyone, and it shouldn't see student work.

What to Provide as Materials

📋 SyllabusFrom practice

Prerequisites, grading policy, schedule, course structure. Lets the assistant answer structural questions accurately instead of guessing.

📊 Slide DecksFrom practice

Defines what's actually testable vs. enrichment. The assistant works from what you emphasized, not just what's in the textbook.

📝 Assignment Instructions & RubricsSuggestion

Without these, an assistant drafting feedback or practice items has to guess at your standards. With them, it can apply your actual criteria.

📚 Textbook InfoFrom practice

Specify edition and chapters covered. Treated as supplementary, not as a replacement for your own materials.

🗣️ Writing SamplesSuggestion

Old lecture notes, prior announcements, graded feedback you've written. If you want drafts to sound like you instead of generic AI, voice comes from examples, not instructions.

🏫 Program ContextSuggestion

Shared learning outcomes or program-level language, if you want consistency across courses in a multi-course pilot.

What stays out

No identifiable student work, no grades, no names or IDs — not even de-identified, unless you've stripped it yourself and confirmed it's clean. This is a course-prep tool, not a grading tool.

The Instruction That Matters Most

Materials alone don't stop an assistant from quietly filling gaps with outside knowledge and presenting it as if it came from your slides. Tell it not to:

Prompt Snippet · From Practice
## How to Use My Materials When answering questions or drafting content, use this priority: 1. My uploaded materials (syllabus, slides, rubrics) = definitive 2. Textbook = supplementary, clearly labeled as such 3. General field knowledge = only when my materials are silent Never blend silently. If you're drawing on anything not explicitly in my materials, say so out loud: "NOT in your materials — adding from general [field] knowledge." Don't reframe outside knowledge to make it sound like it came from my own documents.

This single instruction is the difference between an assistant that extends your materials and one that quietly replaces them with its own.

If you're in the Psych AI Integration Pilot

Boodlebox is the institutionally backed default — FERPA-compliant, with licenses already provisioned through the pilot. Build one folder per course, upload your knowledge sources once, and the same materials can back multiple bots (a workspace assistant for you, a tutor bot for students) without re-uploading.

Create a folder per course

Keep materials organized by course, or by unit if a course has a lot of content. Boodlebox's shared-folder structure means everything in it is available to any bot pointed at that folder.

Upload your knowledge sources once

Syllabus, slides, rubrics, writing samples. The Knowledge Bank persists across bots and chats, so you don't re-upload for every new conversation.

Add the priority instruction above

Paste the "How to Use My Materials" snippet as the bot's custom instructions. This is what keeps it grounded in your materials instead of drifting toward generic answers.

Test it before relying on it

Ask it something you already know the answer to. Check whether it cites your materials or quietly fills in from elsewhere.

Privacy, Briefly

De-identify anything involving students before it goes into any AI tool, consumer or institutional. Boodlebox's FERPA status covers the platform; it doesn't substitute for stripping names and IDs yourself.
